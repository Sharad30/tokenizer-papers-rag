**Chat:**

Tokenizer fertility refers to the average number of tokens into which a word is split during tokenization. It is a measure of how aggressively a tokenizer segments words into subwords. Fertility is calculated by tokenizing a corpus and then averaging the number of tokens generated per word [1]. 

To measure fertility, tokenizers are applied to a dataset, and the average number of tokens per word is computed. For example, the word 'paratrooper' tokenized as ('para', '##tro', '##oper') has a fertility of 3 [2]. Fertility can also be assessed using parallel corpora, such as the Macocu parallel English-Ukrainian dataset, to compare fertility across languages [3]. Additionally, fertility is often evaluated alongside other metrics like the proportion of continued words and Normalized Sequence Length (NSL) to assess tokenizer performance [1].

**You:** Why is it a problem when fertility is high for a language?

*Rewritten for retrieval:* Why is high fertility a problem for a language?

*Best dense cosine:* 0.765

**Chat:**

High fertility is a problem for a language because it leads to greater fragmentation of words into subword tokens, which can obscure productive morphology and reduce the efficiency of tokenization. In morphologically rich languages, very high fertility often reflects near character-level behavior, which can make it harder for models to capture meaningful morphological patterns [1]. Additionally, high fertility increases tokenization costs, as it leads to longer sequences and higher computational demands during training and inference, which can create economic barriers [2]. Furthermore, high fertility is associated with lower reading-comprehension accuracy in some languages, although this relationship is partially explained by resource levels rather than fertility alone [3]. Lastly, high fertility may also contribute to semantic issues, such as "Semantic Hollowing," where reduced token counts do not correspond to richer semantic structures, potentially limiting the model's ability to perform nuanced reasoning [4].
